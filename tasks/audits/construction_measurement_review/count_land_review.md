## Adopted judgments and remaining uncertainty

**The recommendation for the nine unresolved records is to stop trying to manufacture certainty and withhold them.** They are Lawrence, Lake, Circa 922, Wolf Point East, Jackson, 38th, Ohio, the successor at 42nd and the 63rd Street pair. Counts and supported measurements remain in the evidence record. No hand-measured or allocated lot areas are proposed. For 38th and Ohio, missing usable floor area excludes them from the agreed common FAR-and-DUPAC sample even though one dwelling and a reported lot are supported.

**The two Sawyer records are the main count judgment.** Two is recorded consistently after an isolated first count of three, with stable building and lot areas. I favor retaining two, but that history does not prove the original count. This is not a population-wide majority-vote rule.

**Cleveland is a use-definition judgment.** The [operator](https://www.thesonochicago.com/rooms) identifies guest-room lodging; a [2016 visitor's account](https://www.theghostguest.com/2016/10/a-chicago-staycation.html) describes a newly built bed-and-breakfast with owners living on site. I favor exclusion from ordinary residential construction. The single-family permit alone is insufficient to describe its completed use, and the inherited floor number is unsupported in any event.

The date recommendations also distinguish a supported year from an exact certified completion date. In particular, Van Buren uses the earlier Assessor proxy amid inconsistent marketing, and Superior retains 2022 because occupancy by that year is documented. Their case notes preserve those limits.

## What should be general, and what needs a recorded decision?

Review changed apartment counts when building and lot measurements stay fixed. The test should raise a question; it cannot select the true count automatically. Examples show why:

- Madison's later 81 is a community total. The correct individual counts are nine and eighteen; the old 24 at 1048 is also wrong.
- A shop may be counted with apartments, but subtracting one from every mixed-use building would also change correctly recorded counts.
- A later conversion can change the real dwelling count without changing the original construction. Sawyer's documented conversion elsewhere in this set and Division's fitness-center conversion illustrate this.
- A replacement can have an old floor number copied forward. A demolition/new-building permit establishes replacement identity, not the replacement's square footage.
- A shared site can have correct total land but be the wrong denominator for one building.

Use the original completed building as the object of measurement, give source-reported values priority, and record exceptions when evidence identifies the correct value. A construction permit describes authorized work; its issuance or current status does not by itself establish the completed plan or completion year. A property's public-record listing may simply repeat the Assessor and is not a second independent source.

Neither these 87 nor the older 218 flags are a complete inventory of possible errors. This work does not validate every construction record, and the deferred sample of unflagged buildings has not begun.

completion description for 544 W Oak. The permit PIN alone was misleading: its

reported floor/lot. The prior 112/2011 recommendation is superseded.

County street field still says 2904, while its permit and taxpayer mailing
address identify 2910. Each source choice and limitation is in the same
