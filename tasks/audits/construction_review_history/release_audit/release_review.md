## 1. Critical: the score procedure and its interpretation need a decision

The first stage adjusts processing time for controls without alderman indicators. The second stage estimates alderman indicators using those outcome residuals, with lagged permit volume included again. This defines a residual-based descriptive index. It does **not** generally recover the alderman coefficients from a joint regression controlling for those same characteristics. Residualizing the outcome alone is not the Frisch–Waugh–Lovell procedure; with multiple group indicators it can change rankings, not merely their scale. [Hull, On Residualized Outcome Regressions](https://www.mit.edu/~hull/ResOut.pdf) explains the algebra.

Evidence: [stage-one formula](/Users/jacobherbstman/Desktop/aldermanic_privilege/tasks/_lib/alderman_uncertainty_helpers.R:243), [stage-two formula](/Users/jacobherbstman/Desktop/aldermanic_privilege/tasks/_lib/alderman_uncertainty_helpers.R:280), and the manuscript's claim to “isolate” alderman-specific delay in [the conclusion](/Users/jacobherbstman/Desktop/aldermanic_privilege/paper/sections/conclusion.tex:10).

I reproduced the published scores, then jointly estimated alderman effects and **the same controls, on the same complete-case permit sample**. Across all 113 scored aldermen, the raw-effect Pearson correlation is 0.348 and the rank correlation is 0.339. Joint raw effects change the orientation of 1,344/3,692 density observations relative to the current raw effects, or 1,222/3,692 relative to the published shrunken score.

Holding the density regression, observations, and controls fixed gives:

| Score used to orient the boundary | All FAR | All DUPAC | Multifamily FAR | Multifamily DUPAC |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Published, with shrinkage | −0.131 (.047) | −0.174 (.067) | −0.149 (.069) | −0.194 (.099) |
| Current residualized score, before shrinkage | −0.110 (.047) | −0.148 (.065) | −0.132 (.069) | −0.186 (.102) |
| Jointly estimated raw alderman effects | +0.004 (.055) | −0.006 (.076) | −0.159 (.090) | −0.198 (.123) |

Entries are log-point coefficients with ward-pair clustered standard errors. The joint comparison is **not a fully developed replacement estimator**: it omits shrinkage, and joint adjustment does not itself identify a causal politician effect. The middle row separates removing shrinkage from changing the adjustment method. The disappearance of the all-construction differences is sufficiently large that this cannot be dismissed as a technical detail.

Before release, decide whether the intended object is a residual-performance index or a conditional alderman effect. Explain why its variation represents discretionary regulation rather than residual project mix or location. Develop the corresponding estimator and validation independently of which version preserves the desired result. Revisit the through-2014 treatment ranking and prices after that decision; this audit did not substitute the joint score into those two designs.

The predecessor–successor correlation is descriptive support, not an identification test. Different tenures, project composition, score noise, and shrinkage can weaken that correlation even when ward conditions matter.

## 6. Major judgment call: the permit result conditions away an extensive margin

The [main estimator](/Users/jacobherbstman/Desktop/aldermanic_privilege/tasks/run_event_study_permit/code/run_binary_event_study_permit.R:109) requires positive high-discretion activity in 2010–2014. This is disclosed, but the [justification](/Users/jacobherbstman/Desktop/aldermanic_privilege/paper/sections/empirics_results.tex:15)—that a subsequent permit otherwise mechanically appears as an increase—is insufficient. An increase from zero is an economically meaningful part of the development response. PPML handles zero outcomes; it does not require positive pre-treatment counts.

With the same geography, stable-incumbent restriction, treatment, fixed effects, and clustering, admitting those blocks changes the coefficient from −0.1265 (SE .0488; p=.010) to −0.0736 (SE .0471; p=.118). The estimation sample expands from 36,830 to 50,972 block-years after PPML's own zero-group removals. This changes the target population as well as the estimate, so it is not a proof that the original estimate is biased.

Present this sensitivity and describe the baseline as an effect among previously active blocks. Choose the scope on economic grounds. Dropping calendar 2015 itself barely changes the result: −0.1237 (.0454), which is reassuring about mixing pre- and post-May observations in that transition year.

The balance table tests **any reassignment versus no reassignment**, while the principal treatment distinguishes movement toward stringency and leniency. See [balance treatment](/Users/jacobherbstman/Desktop/aldermanic_privilege/tasks/permit_itt_balance/code/build_permit_itt_balance.R:56). Add direction-specific balance checks; favorable pooled balance can conceal opposing differences.

## 7. Major interpretation issue: the causal claims exceed the comparisons

- **Centralization and asymmetry.** The separate estimates are −.231 and +.067, but the paper's own equal-and-opposite test has p=.180. The difference in significance is not evidence of statistically established asymmetry. Neither direction observes a centralized approval regime. The [centralized-counterfactual claim](/Users/jacobherbstman/Desktop/aldermanic_privilege/paper/sections/empirics_results.tex:46) should be bounded accordingly.
- **Low-discretion placebo.** −.017 with SE .050 is not a precise zero: a conventional 95% interval is roughly −11% to +9%. Its pre-period joint test is p=.066. The [introduction's claim](/Users/jacobherbstman/Desktop/aldermanic_privilege/paper/sections/intro.tex:33) that this “shows” discretionary authority rather than general efficiency is too strong. Test the high-minus-low response directly, accounting for covariance, rather than comparing their individual significance.
- **Permits versus entry.** The data record issued permits, classified by application-start year; they do not contain all attempted or abandoned applications. The [City's dataset documentation](https://data.cityofchicago.org/Buildings/Building-Permits/ydr8-5enu) describes issued permits. Issuance, cancellation, and delayed appearance in the extract can all affect counts. Call the outcome application-dated issued permits, and distinguish it from underlying project entry.
- **Density conditional on completion.** Lower density among completed projects need not equal a within-project reduction in planned scale. Stringency can change which projects complete. June 15 of completion year may assign a different alderman from the one involved in entitlement or approval. Both issues warrant explicit limits and, when provenance is restored, approval-date or stable-through-development sensitivities.
- **Formal zoning.** A broad construction-date zoning-group FE does not hold detailed permitted FAR, height, unit limits, overlays, or individual planned-development rules fixed. Zoning may itself have changed through aldermanic action. Avoid interpreting this as a separately identified effect holding all formal rules constant.
- **Prices and affordability.** The price regression compares selected transactions conditional on observed housing characteristics. It does not identify citywide affordability or rule out demand, neighborhood sorting, or displacement across the boundary. The paper already calls prices suggestive; retain that qualification consistently.

The 100-ft-bin estimator is faithfully implemented and can remain the main estimator. Its adjacent-bin contrast is a finite-band comparison, however, rather than an estimated limit at distance zero. ## 9. Moderate unresolved inference issue: score uncertainty is omitted

The stage-two shrinkage variances use heteroskedasticity-robust ward-month errors conditional on stage-one residuals, while downstream estimates condition on estimated scores and their binary ordering. Neither accounts for the full uncertainty in estimating the adjustment model and classifying the stricter endpoint. Fixed score-gap cutoffs are useful sensitivity checks but are not confidence statements about pair rankings. Develop repeated-sample or out-of-sample validation appropriate to the chosen score method.

Ward-pair clustering also treats pairs sharing a ward as independent. [Aronow, Samii and Assenova](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.3398) describe covariance estimators for shared endpoints. I implemented that sandwich diagnostic, checked its ordinary pair-cluster component against `fixest`, and reported both uncorrected standard errors and a version using the baseline scalar correction. It does **not** uniformly increase uncertainty here: the sales SE falls from .0134 to about .0089, while all-construction DUPAC rises from .0669 to about .0767. Thus this check does not substantiate a claim that pair clustering alone creates the headline significance. It also does not resolve generated-score uncertainty, spatial dependence beyond shared wards, or finite-network inference. Do not automatically choose whichever covariance estimator is smaller.
