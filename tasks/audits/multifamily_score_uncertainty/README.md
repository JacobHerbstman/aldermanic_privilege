# Multifamily density with uncertain score orderings

This audit carries the saved score draws through the paper's multifamily FAR and DUPAC specifications, and examines sensitivity to dropping or reversing local alderman comparisons. Run `make` in `code/` and open `output/multifamily_score_sensitivity.html`. Two scripts estimate the models and display the results. Production data, the chosen score method, and the manuscript are unchanged.

## Inputs and comparison

The construction input is the recorded common FAR/DUPAC sample in `data_raw/score_robustness/`; its producer and hash are recorded in `../score_pair_reliability/sources/benchmark_sources.csv`. Scores, resampling draws, and pair retention shares are concrete Make dependencies on `../score_pair_reliability/output/`. The three resampling methods and their limitations are documented in that upstream audit. This consumer does not re-estimate scores or alter their weights.

The baseline uses multifamily buildings completed in 2006–2022, positive valid FAR and DUPAC under the existing common eligibility flag, complete own-ward controls, and distance strictly below 500 feet. There are ten 100-foot distance bins. The coefficient compares [0,100) on the more-stringent side with [-100,0) on the less-stringent side. Controls are the five submitted own-ward demographics; fixed effects are zoning group, boundary segment and construction year; standard errors are clustered by geographic ward pair. The estimator verifies that both density outcomes use the same fitted rows. It also verifies that the source scores reproduce the recorded signed-distance direction and that reversing every boundary negates both coefficients and preserves their standard errors to numerical tolerance.

## Three questions

1. **What happens under the full score draws?** Each of the 499 property, 499 quarter and 499 annual draws reorders all boundaries using its complete alderman score vector. All buildings, outcomes, locations, distances in absolute value, controls and fixed effects stay fixed. All observed pairs must have nonzero finite score gaps; any missing or tied assignment stops the run. These are coherent citywide rankings. The results record both fitted coefficients, their conditional clustered standard errors, and the number of pairs/buildings whose side changes. The median and middle 95% of coefficients describe score uncertainty only, not combined outcome uncertainty or a formal confidence interval for the treatment effect. Negative-draw shares are descriptive, not probabilities that the true causal effect is negative.

2. **What happens if uncertain pairs are dropped or reversed together?** Define fragility relative to the baseline ordering: retention below 75%, and separately below 95%, under each resampling method. Both thresholds are declared in Make and chosen before inspecting density results. Each selected set is first dropped in full, then reversed in full while retaining all buildings. Dropping a set changes the population represented by the regression; it does not automatically produce a corrected estimate. Reversing selected edges can conflict with a globally coherent ranking, so these cases are deliberately labeled stress tests. They are not adopted scores. Annual classification is displayed first, with both other classifications available in the same report.

3. **Does a particular pair drive the result?** Drop every one of the 161 observed alderman pairs in turn, then reverse each pair in turn, for both outcomes. The full table retains all pairs, including stable ones. The report displays the ten largest absolute changes in either log coefficient as an influence diagnostic, not a selection rule for a preferred regression. Changes from nonlinear percentage transformations are not additive contributions. Every pair represents the two named aldermen; different incumbents along one geographic boundary remain separate comparisons.

## Outputs and interpretation

`multifamily_score_draws.csv` preserves every refit using the saved coherent score vectors. `multifamily_pair_sensitivity.csv` contains the baseline, all grouped stress tests, and every one-pair test. `multifamily_pairs.csv` records building counts, geographic ward pairs, baseline ordering, and retention under all three score methods. The renderer produces the draw summary, the joined pair-influence table, the figure, and the HTML report. SaveData writes reports as side effects; logs and reports are not Make targets.

The quoted conditional intervals and p-values for a dropped/reversed specification treat that specification and its ordering as fixed. They do not account for using estimated scores to define the selected set. No result in this audit combines score uncertainty with resampled building outcomes. The experiments preserve the previously chosen sequential score construction and do not resolve its sensitivity to jointly estimating neighborhood controls and alderman effects.
