# Campaign donations and permit stringency

This is an exploratory comparison on `score_robustness`. It asks whether campaign funding composition ranks aldermen similarly to the submitted permit scores, and whether those donation rankings persist over time. Agreement is not a validation test with known truth: donors may support politicians who share their preferences, seek access to restrictive politicians, or respond to local development opportunities.

Run `make` from `code/`. Open `output/donation_review.html`. The three scripts have separate, visible jobs:

1. `clean_donations.R`: read the recorded receipts, identify recipients, remove superseded filings, and classify donations.
2. `compare_donations.R`: aggregate the declared donation measures and compare rankings, periods, and recipients.
3. `render_donation_review.R`: present the saved comparisons and charts.

The submitted scores and manuscript are not consumers of this audit. The score files are pinned benchmarks, so running this comparison cannot reestimate the submitted score.

## Cleaning, in order

**Receipt and recipient identity.** One output row is one non-superseded ISBE receipt ID. The recipient mapping must assign each committee ID to one person. The earlier mapping was assembled around election winners. Its personal-campaign flag defines the baseline; the broader named-committee flag is a sensitivity check. A unique Chicago registry committee containing a missing scored official's full name and a candidate cue (`friends`, `citizens`, or `elect`) recovers three omitted appointees. Ambiguous or absent matches stay unmatched. Both mapping sources remain visible on each receipt. Committee attribution can extend beyond an alderman's tenure; only the baseline's in-office restriction removes those dates.

**Parse the source without inventing repairs.** The official export has 29 tab-separated fields. Exclude physical rows with a different width or a nonnumeric receipt ID. There are 31 malformed physical rows in the complete source; none belongs to the included committee IDs. Mixed text encoding is decoded to UTF-8. The cleaner preserves source row numbers, filing IDs, and original donor/job fields. Names are lowercased, ampersands become `and`, and punctuation becomes spaces for matching. No fuzzy personal-name matching is performed.

**Amendments and money.** `Archived = true` means a receipt was superseded by an amended filing, according to the ISBE dictionary; those rows are excluded. The baseline retains positive, dated individual contributions and committee transfers (`1A`, `2A`). An explicit alternative adds in-kind contributions (`5A`). Loans and other receipt types are outside the measure. Sum `Amount`, never `AggregateAmount`; do not collapse different receipt IDs merely because dates and amounts coincide. Missing/invalid amounts remain exclusions, not zeros. Blank quoted classification cells are explicitly converted to missing values and category membership is checked.

**Own-committee transfers.** The baseline excludes a transfer only when its normalized donor name exactly matches an alias of another recorded committee belonging to the same recipient. The `gross_transfers` comparison keeps these. This catches recognizable internal transfers; it does not claim to identify every abbreviated alias or trace funds through unrelated intermediaries. All other transfers remain in both numerator and denominator when appropriate.

**Recorded union judgments.** Join the old donor-name classification once; apply the old ordered name rules to unclassified names, preserving explicit recorded exclusions. The old table has 189 preliminary accepts and two preliminary excludes awaiting a spot-check, plus 27 auto-seeded accepts awaiting review. The `union_direct_no_pending` measure excludes the 27 auto-seeded names only; it is not a fully human-verified subset. `union_original_rules` retains the older vocabulary before the two new union-family rules.

**Organizations versus individuals.** A direct union contribution requires a classified union category, a blank individual first-name field, and an organizational cue in the donor name. Union PACs belong here as well as union organizations. Separately report classified individuals and other union-linked records; their donations must not be described as gifts from a union treasury. The organizational screen is conservative and can miss an organization entered in individual-name fields. The legacy classification may use employment information pooled across receipts; it is retained as a comparison, not assumed to prove affiliation on every date.

**Named additions.** The old automatic vocabulary omitted Teamsters and the Fraternal Order of Police. Full donor-name rules now classify these as generic labor and service/public-sector organizations, respectively. Teamsters are not automatically construction trades. The evidence table also recognizes ten company families: nine real-estate developers and the Walsh contractor family. Company patterns require distinctive company names; personal surnames alone, bare `Magellan`, and ambiguous abbreviations do not qualify. A match comes from the donor name or the employer recorded on that receipt, not a donor's future employer.

**Real estate and development.** The script keeps the following definitions separately so their effects can be inspected:

- `real_estate_strict`: literal real-estate, realty, realtor, property-management, or landlord wording in the donor, occupation, or employer fields.
- `developer_explicit`: wording explicitly linking development to property, housing, residential/commercial real estate, or homebuilding.
- `developer_verified`: explicit development wording plus the documented developer company names.
- `real_estate_verified`: the union of the strict real-estate and verified developer definitions. This includes firms and people whose recorded jobs/employers identify a link; it is not exclusively developer executives.
- `real_estate_broad`: the preceding definition plus ambiguous property/development wording, excluding identified software, business, economic, community, and child-development contexts. This is a sensitivity definition, not a preferred classification.
- `estate_name_evidence`: real-estate/developer wording or a documented company in the donor-name field; personal employer information alone does not qualify.
- `nonunion_construction`: private contractors/homebuilders, separately from union organizations. “Nonunion” here means the donor is not classified as a union organization; it does not mean the contractor employs no union workers.
- `land_use_professional`: architects and explicit zoning/land-use occupations. Ordinary lawyers and generic financiers are not included without that evidence.
- `development_coalition`: real estate/developers, private construction, or construction-trade organizations, counting a receipt once even if several categories apply. This is a broad economic hypothesis, not an inferred common policy preference.

No external information about wealthy individuals alone turns their donations into developer donations. Unidentified LLCs, vague job fields, missing employers, abbreviations, and homonyms remain limitations. Sector groups overlap: their shares should not be added together. A donor-level review table summarizes observed classifications; the calculations use receipt-level classifications, never the table's across-history `any` flags.

## Comparison definitions

The main observation is one alderman with observed eligible receipts while in office during 2006–2022. The denominator is the sum of recorded eligible itemized contributions and transfers, **not all campaign resources**. Shares include zero sector receipts if eligible fundraising is observed. No matched committee, no observed fundraising, or no observed in-office months is missing, not a zero donation share.

Each sector has six measurements: dollar share, share after annual CPI adjustment, receipt-count share, distinct donor-name share, real sector dollars per year, and dollar share after removing the largest donor. Donor names are not perfectly identified people: spelling variations can split a donor and homonyms can combine donors. All tied largest donor names are removed in the last measure. CPI amounts are expressed in 2022 dollars. In-office annual amounts divide by recorded office months/12, including months with no donations.

Compare personal committees during actual office months, personal committees throughout the calendar window, the broader recorded named committees during office months, inclusion of in-kind gifts, inclusion of recognizable own transfers, and the older election-cycle assignment. The older cycle convention assigns the prior four years of contributions to the eventual elected alderman; it is not an in-office measure. The original pooled 1999–2023 output is shown separately from a reaggregation restricted to 2006–2022.

The correlations report each available sample and a separate common group observed under every main-period recipient definition. The latter holds the people fixed when comparing definitions. Pairwise donation-definition agreement also reports its own matched N.

The donation time split is 2006–2013 versus 2014–2022 for the same people observed in both periods. Wider histories cover 1994–2005 and 2023–2025, plus **partial 2026** separately; these track the same recorded people/committees and may include other offices. The saved roster does not establish post-2022 alderman tenure. Donations run through August 1, 2026 and CPI through June; the 2026 totals and CPI mean are incomplete and are not extrapolated.

Pearson correlations compare levels; Spearman correlations compare ranks. All declared definitions are retained. Scores are compared in their original direction: a negative correlation means higher sector donations are associated with less stringency. The hypothesis that development-linked giving signals leniency is explicit; no sign is flipped or weighting chosen to maximize agreement. The early score is the nested submitted score through 2014, not an independently estimated late-period score. Approximate Pearson confidence intervals treat observed alderman pairs as independent and do not incorporate score estimation error. These are descriptive exploratory comparisons, not causal estimates or confirmatory significance tests.
