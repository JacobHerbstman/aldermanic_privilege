\section{Additional Permit Evidence}
\label{sec:appendix_permits}

This appendix reports additional permit evidence that complements the descriptive discussion in Section~\ref{sec:data} and the event study in Section~\ref{sec:empirical_results_permits}.

\subsection{Balance Before Redistricting}

Table~\ref{tab:permit_itt_balance} compares blocks that changed wards in 2015 with nearby blocks that remained in the same ward.
Before the new map took effect, the two groups were broadly similar in permit activity and neighborhood characteristics.
The joint test across all listed characteristics has a $p$-value of 0.912.

\subsection{Permit Event-Study Estimates}

Table~\ref{tab:permit_event_study_did} reports pooled estimates for years 0 through 5 after redistricting.
It shows the main model, which treats moves toward greater stringency and greater leniency as opposite changes, and a more flexible model that estimates each direction separately.
Low-discretion permits show a more muted response.

\subsection{Allowing the Two Reassignment Directions to Differ}
\label{sec:appendix_permit_unconstrained}

Figure~\ref{fig:permit_event_study_unconstrained} allows moves toward greater stringency and greater leniency to have different effects.
One-half of the difference between those two effects is nearly identical to the main estimate.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.7\textwidth]{../tasks/run_event_study_permit/output/permit_event_study_high_discretion_stable_separate_500ft.pdf}
    \caption{Permit Event Study Allowing the Two Directions to Differ}
    \figurenotes{The model estimates separate event-time coefficients for blocks assigned toward more stringent and more lenient aldermen.
    Each point converts one-half of the difference between those log coefficients to a proportional change using $\exp(\cdot)-1$.
    Shaded areas are 95\% confidence intervals based on standard errors clustered by ward pair.
    The pooled log contrast is $-0.146$ (SE $=0.050$).
    The equal-and-opposite test for the pooled coefficients has $p=0.179$, and the joint pre-trend test has $p=0.465$.}
    \label{fig:permit_event_study_unconstrained}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:permit_event_study_low_discretion} shows the full event-study path for low-discretion permits.

\subsection{Broader Assigned-Change Sample}
\label{sec:appendix_permit_assigned}

Figure~\ref{fig:permit_event_study_assigned} includes blocks from wards that elected new aldermen in 2015.
Treatment remains based on the 2014 origin- and destination-ward incumbents, so the estimate captures assigned rather than necessarily realized changes in stringency.
The annual number of high-discretion permits is 8.3\% lower, a somewhat smaller estimate than in the main sample.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.7\textwidth]{../tasks/run_event_study_permit/output/permit_event_study_high_discretion_all_signed_500ft.pdf}
    \caption{Permit Event Study: Broader Assigned-Change Sample}
    \figurenotes{The specification matches Figure~\ref{fig:permit_event_study}, but the sample also includes blocks from wards with aldermanic turnover in 2015.
    Treatment remains defined by the 2014 origin- and destination-ward incumbents.
    Shaded areas are 95\% confidence intervals based on standard errors clustered by ward pair.
    The pooled estimate is $-0.087$ (SE $=0.036$), implying that the annual number of recorded permits is 8.3\% lower on average in years 0--5.
    Pre-trend test $p=0.684$.}
    \label{fig:permit_event_study_assigned}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Permit Summary Statistics}
\label{app:permit_summary_stats}

Table~\ref{tab:permit_summary_stats} compares the high- and low-discretion permit groups defined in Section~\ref{subsec:permit_pipeline}.
They are much slower than lower-discretion permits: the mean processing time is 57.3 days versus 26.2 days, and the median is 30 days versus 6 days.
They also display more cross-alderman variation in processing times.
