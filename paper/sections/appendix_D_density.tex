\section{Additional Density RD Checks}
\label{sec:appendix_density}

This appendix checks whether the density result appears at false cutoffs, reflects fixed differences in location, or depends on unusual boundary geometry or projects closest to the boundary.
Unless otherwise noted, each estimate compares the two 100ft bands immediately across the ward boundary using Equation~\eqref{eq:segment_fe}.

\subsection{Stringency-Score Checks}

The first check asks whether a project's own permits influence the score used to classify its boundary.
For each project, I recalculate the scores for the two aldermen after removing permits matched to that project.
The estimates are effectively unchanged.
The second check drops boundaries where the two alderman scores are close.
All four point estimates remain negative when the minimum gap is either 0.25 or 0.50 standard deviations, although the multifamily estimates become less precise as the sample shrinks.

\subsection{Boundary Geometry}

Some ward boundaries follow expressways, waterways, arterial roads, or other physical features.
Table~\ref{tab:density_boundary_robustness} therefore repeats the analysis after restricting the sample based on the geometry of the assigned boundary segment.
Removing segments with substantial expressway or water overlap leaves the estimates almost unchanged.
A broader physical-feature and arterial-road restriction attenuates the multifamily estimates, while the all-construction estimates remain negative.
Restricting the sample to locally straight boundaries also produces four negative estimates.
These estimates are generally smaller and less precise in the reduced sample, but they do not suggest that boundary curvature alone creates the main pattern.
