\section{Additional Density Boundary Checks}
\label{sec:appendix_density}

This appendix asks whether the density result could be an artifact of how I measure aldermanic stringency or where ward boundaries happen to lie.
It also tests for jumps away from true boundaries and checks whether projects immediately next to a boundary drive the result.
Unless otherwise noted, each estimate compares the two 100~ft bands immediately across the ward boundary using Equation~\eqref{eq:segment_fe}.

\subsection{Stringency-Score Checks}
\label{subsec:density_stringency_checks}

The first check asks whether a project's own permits influence the score used to classify its boundary.
For each project, I recalculate the scores for the two aldermen after removing permits matched to that project.
The estimates are effectively unchanged.
The second check drops boundaries where the two aldermen receive similar scores and the more-stringent side is therefore harder to distinguish.
The estimates of FAR and units per acre remain negative in both construction samples when the required score difference is either 0.25 or 0.50 standard deviations, although the multifamily estimates become less precise as the sample shrinks.

\subsection{Placebo Cutoffs}
\label{subsec:density_placebo_cutoffs}

Figure~\ref{fig:rd_placebo_shifts} moves the cutoff 1000~ft inside either side of the true ward boundary.
These locations do not mark a change in aldermanic authority.
Across the two outcomes and two construction samples, none of the estimated differences across an artificial cutoff is statistically distinguishable from zero.

\subsection{Boundary Geometry}
\label{subsec:density_boundary_geometry}

Some ward boundaries follow expressways, waterways, arterial roads, or other physical features.
Table~\ref{tab:density_boundary_robustness} therefore repeats the analysis after restricting the sample based on the geometry of the assigned boundary segment.
Removing segments with substantial expressway or water overlap leaves the estimates almost unchanged.
A broader physical-feature and arterial-road restriction attenuates the multifamily estimates, while the all-construction estimates remain negative.
Restricting the sample to locally straight boundaries also produces negative estimates for FAR and units per acre in both construction samples.
These estimates are generally smaller and less precise in the reduced sample, but they do not suggest that boundary curvature alone creates the main pattern.
