\section{Data and Sample Construction}
\label{app:data_sources}
\label{sec:appendix_data}

This appendix provides additional detail on the data sources and sample rules described in Section~\ref{sec:data}.

\subsection{Data Sources}

The property data come from Cook County Assessor sales, assessment, parcel, and improvement files.
RentHub provides rental listings from 2014--2022.
Building permits and ward boundaries come from the City of Chicago.
The amenity controls use school locations, Chicago Park District boundaries, major streets, CTA stops, and Lake Michigan geometry; CTA distances use the permanent station network in place during the listing month or on the sale date.

Demographic controls come from American Community Survey block groups and 2010 and 2020 census blocks.
I allocate population, households, tenure, race, ethnicity, and education counts to wards according to the share of each block group's area falling within the relevant ward boundaries.
Ward shares use those allocated counts, while ward median household income is the household-weighted mean of block group medians.
When historical ward polygons overlap slightly, I scale the intersections to the block group's area inside the union of Chicago wards so no area is counted twice.

\subsection{High-Discretion Permits}
\label{subsec:permits_sample}

The permit records cover Department of Buildings permits since 2006.
The high-discretion group contains the permit types defined in Section~\ref{subsec:permit_pipeline}; low-discretion placebo permits are the remaining routine types, excluding signs.
The event-study outcomes omit records marked cancelled, revoked, or suspended.

Processing time is the number of days between application and issuance.
The processing-time regression excludes missing dates, negative durations, and same-day permits; same-day permits remain in the lagged ward-month permit count.
I assign each permit to the ward containing its coordinates under the boundaries in force on its application date.

\subsection{Permit Event-Study Panel}
\label{subsec:event_study_panel}

The treatment panel compares each census block's ward under the pre-2015 and post-2015 maps.
Blocks are projected to EPSG:3435 and assigned by largest-area overlap.
Blocks moved to a different ward are classified using the difference between the destination and origin aldermen's stringency scores:
\[
\Delta\text{Stringency}_i = \text{Stringency}_{\text{dest}} - \text{Stringency}_{\text{origin}}.
\]
Positive values indicate assignment toward greater stringency, negative values indicate assignment toward greater leniency, and unchanged blocks form the comparison group.

For this design, stringency scores use permits filed from 2006 through 2014.
Both origin and destination scores are assigned using the incumbents serving in 2014.
The main sample retains blocks only when both incumbents remained in office when the new map took effect, so treatment matches the change the block experienced without using the outcome of the 2015 election.
Online Appendix C.4 adds wards with electoral turnover and interprets the resulting estimate as assigned rather than necessarily realized stringency.

Permits are aggregated by block and application year for relative years $-5$ through $5$.
The sample includes blocks whose polygon centroid is within 500ft of a pre-redistricting boundary.
Treated blocks use the fixed origin-destination boundary created by redistricting; comparison blocks use their nearest incident pre-redistricting boundary.
The estimation sample requires at least one high-discretion permit during years $-5$ through $-1$.

\subsection{New Construction}
\label{subsec:new_construction_sample}

The construction sample combines Cook County residential improvement records with commercial valuation records for properties assessed under multifamily model groups.
I link records across parcel divisions, replacement PINs, and the two Assessor sources so that each project enters once.
I then compare the resulting sample with every issued residential new-construction permit, adding completed projects absent from the Assessor files when units, building area, and lot area can be measured consistently.

Projects must have positive lot area, positive building area, at least one dwelling unit, and a construction year from 2006 through 2022.
Assessor histories sometimes report different years or repeat whole-building characteristics across assessment cards.
I use permit and completion dates to select the year that best matches completion, combine cards that clearly describe one building, and remove later PINs representing the same project.
For 52 projects citywide, including 26 within 500ft of a ward boundary, the administrative records do not fully resolve the case; the replication files include a spreadsheet recording each decision.

Locations first come from the Historical Parcel Universe in the construction year.
When the original PIN is unavailable there, I use the current location of that PIN or a verified historical address.
Each project retains one location, preventing predecessor and successor PINs from entering separately.
Multifamily status primarily follows Assessor property class.
When the reported unit count does not plausibly describe the whole building, permit descriptions and other property records determine the whole-building count, with the remaining decisions recorded in the same spreadsheet.

Each project receives the broad zoning group in force on June 15 of its construction year.
The zoning history combines archived City maps and passed map amendments and uses historical parcel boundaries to follow PIN changes.
FAR is total building area divided by lot area; DUPAC is dwelling units divided by lot acreage.
Projects are assigned to the ward map and alderman in office on June 15 of the reported construction year because the Assessor does not provide an exact completion date.
All ward assignment and distance calculations use EPSG:3435, with distance reported in feet.

\subsection{Home Sales}
\label{subsec:sales_sample}

The sales sample covers Chicago transactions from 1999--2022, with the analysis restricted to 2006--2022.
It includes residential classes 202--211 but excludes mixed-use properties and condominiums.
Sales must exceed \$10,000, use a warranty or trustee deed, include recorded and non-identical seller and buyer names, and involve a single parcel; land-only transactions are excluded.
Prices are deflated to 2022 dollars using the monthly Chicago CPI-U and winsorized at the 1st and 99th percentiles of the analysis sample.

I geocode each sale by matching its full PIN to the Historical Parcel Universe in the sale year, falling back to the 2025 location of the same PIN when necessary.
Ward and alderman assignments use the exact sale date: transactions before May 18, 2015 use the old map, and those on or after that date use the new map.
Signed distances are calculated in EPSG:3435 and oriented by relative aldermanic stringency.

\subsection{RentHub Listings}
\label{subsec:renthub_sample}

The rental data begin as scrape-level listings.
I standardize addresses and building types and treat zero, blank, NA, N/A, NULL, NONE, and UNKNOWN addresses as missing.
Because the data do not identify units, a property proxy combines normalized address and rounded coordinates, using coordinates alone when address is missing.
A floorplan proxy adds bedrooms, bathrooms, square feet, and building type.
I collapse repeated scrapes to the median rent for each floorplan and scrape date and then to the median daily rent for each floorplan-month.
Before monthly aggregation, rent and square footage are trimmed at the 1st and 99th percentiles within year-by-bedroom cells.

The sample requires positive rent, valid coordinates, and assignment to a Chicago ward.
Ward and alderman assignments use the listing's first observed date and split May 2015 at May 18.
The main analysis retains only reliable locations: it excludes manually rejected coordinates, unstable address clusters, addressless listings concentrated at generic coordinates, and cases where the address's modal coordinate changes the ward, neighboring ward, ward pair, or measured boundary distance by more than 100ft.
Regressions also require square footage, bathrooms, and amenity controls.
Bedrooms enter categorically, so studios remain in the sample.
