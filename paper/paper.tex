\begin{abstract}
Formal zoning regulations are only one part of the regulatory environment developers face.
I study informal regulatory discretion in Chicago, where ``aldermanic privilege'' gives city council members substantial influence over development in their wards.
I construct alderman-level ``stringency scores'' from permit processing times and use complementary event-study and boundary-discontinuity designs.
Assignment to a more stringent alderman lowers high-discretion permitting activity by 12\%, while multifamily density is 14–18\% lower immediately across ward boundaries on the more-stringent side.
I also find suggestive evidence of higher home prices and rents.
Informal regulatory discretion can constrain housing supply even where formal rules are similar.
\end{abstract}
