\section*{Permit event-study sensitivity to uncertain score orderings}
\textit{September 14, 2026. Agent-drafted exploratory checks requested by Jacob.}

The main permit result is more stable under score resampling than the
multifamily density results, although some deliberate pair reversals weaken
it substantially. The original high-discretion signed Poisson estimate is
$-11.9\%$ ($p=0.011$). The fitted panel contains 36,830 block-years, 3,379
blocks and 95 geographic boundary pairs; 687 blocks were reassigned across
44 informative alderman pairs. The audit reproduces the recorded pooled
estimate, every annual coefficient, standard errors, and the joint pretrend
test to numerical precision. It preserves the stable-incumbent sample,
2010--2020 period, 500-foot limit, positive pre-remap permit-volume rule,
block and boundary-pair-by-year fixed effects, and boundary-pair clustering.
The scores use permits through 2014 only.

Using the same thresholds as the multifamily audit, 11 informative pairs
retain their ordering in fewer than 75\% of annual draws. They contain 191
reassigned blocks. Dropping their reassigned and unchanged comparison blocks
makes the pooled estimate $-16.7\%$ ($p=0.0002$); reversing their treatment
directions while keeping every block gives $-12.2\%$ ($p=0.021$). At the
broader 95\% threshold, 21 pairs and 293 reassigned blocks are affected:
dropping gives $-19.9\%$ and reversing gives $-13.4\%$. All four annual
stress tests retain negative post-remap point estimates in every event year.
Their joint pretrend $p$-values range from 0.283 to 0.768, compared with
0.400 originally. These tests do not establish parallel trends.

All three methods' complete score draws produce predominantly negative
pooled estimates. Across 499 draws per method, median effects are $-11.3\%$
for property weights, $-11.0\%$ for quarter weights and $-11.4\%$ for annual
weights. Their middle 95\% ranges are respectively $[-16.0,-5.2]\%$,
$[-16.6,-3.2]\%$ and $[-16.8,-5.5]\%$. All property and annual draws and
99.4\% of quarter draws are negative. These are score-only perturbation
ranges, not full confidence intervals; negative shares are not probabilities
that the causal effect is negative. Each draw supplies one coherent citywide
ranking, and observed permit outcomes remain fixed. The annual exercise has
only nine pre-remap time blocks.

Individual comparisons still matter. Dropping each of the 44 informative
pairs in turn yields effects between $-13.1\%$ and $-9.7\%$. Moreno--Maldonado,
with 58 reassigned blocks, has the largest influence: dropping it gives
$-9.7\%$ ($p=0.060$), and reversing it gives $-4.2\%$ ($p=0.528$). Its
ordering survives 97.4\% of annual draws and 94.4\% of quarter draws.
Dowell--Cochran, with 43 reassigned blocks and only 55.7\% annual retention,
gives $-10.1\%$ when dropped and $-7.5\%$ when reversed. Moreno--Waguespack,
with 56 reassigned blocks and 51.7\% annual retention, gives $-10.5\%$ when
reversed. No individual deletion or reversal changes the pooled sign.

One broader stress test nearly eliminates the result: reversing all 26 pairs
with less than 95\% quarter retention gives $-0.9\%$ ($p=0.894$). This set
contains 425 reassigned blocks, including Moreno--Maldonado. Dropping those
pairs instead gives $-16.9\%$ ($p=0.009$). The full report retains this
unfavorable case alongside every other test. Flipping a selected set is a
deliberate stress test that may conflict with a single citywide ranking;
it is different from using the coherent resampled score vectors. Dropping
changes the population represented. Conditional p-values treat each tested
sample and ordering as fixed.
