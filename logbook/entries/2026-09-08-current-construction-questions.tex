That conflict remains open. Cirrus and Cascade likewise need building-specific treatment; the existing source records must not become three towers.

This is a task for a general location rule, not a request for 2,078 manual decisions. \paragraph{Follow-up: a simpler individual-lot location rule.}
The 2,078 flags do not establish that every historical parcel was larger: the flag identifies a different historical parcel number without an accepted individual point. A later exact-parcel point is available for 2,042 of these records. For 2,009, the 2025 assessment has exactly the selected construction year, units, floor area and reported land area, and the individual parcel point lies inside its historical site. This supports using the later individual-lot point for the unchanged property, while continuing to use the construction-year ward map. It does not replace source-reported land with map area. This is a tested recommendation; production locations have not yet changed.

Relative to the reconstructed historical-parcel centers, these 2,009 points move a median of 27.3 feet. Eleven records enter the 500-foot band and 22 leave; none changes ward. Of the 751 records matched by exact project identifier to the frozen paper input, none enters or leaves that band. This comparison does not test regression coefficients or unmatched records. Most matched distances already agree closely with the later individual-lot points.

The strict rule leaves 69 records: 36 without a usable current exact-parcel point, 21 requiring a measurement check, ten with a different recorded construction year, and two whose points fall less than 0.00002 feet outside the historical polygon. The last two are numerical boundary precision cases, not evidence of a materially different location. The recommendation is a general coordinate rule for unchanged properties, with unresolved cases retained for review rather than assigned the center of a potentially larger former site.
