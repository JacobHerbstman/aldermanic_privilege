\section*{Donation-based stringency and the largest switches}
\textit{September 14, 2026. Agent-drafted correction and follow-up using Jacob's stated interpretation.}

Jacob clarified that more developer funding means less stringent. The previous entries compared raw funding with permit stringency, so their same/reversed labels described the opposite orientation. The exploratory code and report now use donation-based stringency equal to minus the developer share. Under this definition, 20 pairs containing 417 of 583 reassigned blocks reverse their stringency ordering; 17 pairs containing 166 blocks retain it. The main donation-based permit estimate is $+12.0\%$ ($p=0.038$) toward greater stringency, the reverse comparison of $-10.7\%$ toward higher funding. This conflicts with the paper's negative stringency effect under the alternative proxy. Source receipts, classifications, and sample eligibility are unchanged.

By reassigned-block count, the five largest switches are Moreno--Maldonado (wards 1--26, 58 blocks), Moreno--Waguespack (1--32, 56), O'Connor--Pawar (40--47, 46), Dowell--Cochran (3--20, 43), and Ervin--Mitts (28--37, 41). Together they account for 244 of the 417 reversed blocks. Permit stringency ranks Moreno above Maldonado and Waguespack, Pawar above O'Connor, Cochran above Dowell, and Ervin above Mitts. The donation-based interpretation reverses each comparison because those same aldermen have larger developer shares.

Moreno--Maldonado and Dowell--Cochran have the greatest influence on the contrary-sign result. Omitting the first pair gives $+8.7\%$ ($p=0.158$); omitting the second gives $+9.4\%$ ($p=0.089$), compared with the pooled $+12.0\%$. Omitting O'Connor--Pawar gives $+10.8\%$. Moreno--Waguespack is large by block count but offsets the positive result: omitting it gives $+13.0\%$. Its permit-score gap is only 0.023 (0.323 versus 0.300). Cochran's permit-score lead over Dowell is also modest, 0.068 (0.587 versus 0.519). These are observed score differences, not tests that the aldermen's underlying behavior differs.

The receipt-level review shows concentrated developer funding. Moreno's \$8,250 includes one \$5,000 gift from Andy Gloor of Sterling Bay; Maldonado's \$5,000 consists of five gifts from Hipolito Roldan, with Hispanic Housing Development Corp. recorded as employer. Excluding only the Gloor gift as an arithmetic illustration would reduce Moreno's share from 1.00\% to 0.395\%, below Maldonado's 0.571\%. No gift is removed. Pawar's \$3,500 comes from three gifts by David Brown and James Jann; no developer gifts are identified for O'Connor under the current rules. Cochran's \$4,000 consists of two gifts, while Dowell's \$3,000 is recorded under Habitat Company names. Ervin has \$4,000 across four gifts and Mitts has one \$500 gift. These small numerators and zero classifications limit how confidently the funding shares can rank regulatory preferences.
