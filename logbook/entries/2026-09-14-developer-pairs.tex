\section*{Which ward pairs explain the developer result?}
\textit{September 14, 2026. Agent-drafted follow-up to the developer-ranking comparison.}

\textit{Orientation note: same/reversed below compares raw funding with permit stringency. Those labels reverse under Jacob's donation-based stringency definition; see ``Donation-based stringency and the largest switches.''}

The similar negative permit estimates mainly arise because the two rankings agree on the comparisons doing most of the work. Twenty ward pairs contain 417 of the 583 reassigned blocks, or 71.5\%, whose ordering stays the same. In these pairs, the alderman with more identified developer funding also has higher measured permit stringency. A model allowing this group its own effect estimates $-12.9\%$ ($p=0.014$) under either ranking. The other 17 pairs contain 166 reassigned blocks; their estimate is $+5.5\%$ under the original ranking and $-5.2\%$ under developer funding ($p=0.635$ in both cases). The two groups' coefficients were estimated jointly with the original fixed effects and ward-pair clustering. Their percentage effects need not change sign symmetrically because the underlying log coefficients are exponentiated.

The strongest supporting pairs are Moreno--Maldonado (wards 1--26, 58 reassigned blocks) and Dowell--Cochran (3--20, 43 blocks). Moreno's identified developer share is 1.00\%, versus Maldonado's 0.57\%, and Moreno has the higher permit score. Cochran's share is 1.02\%, versus Dowell's 0.30\%, and Cochran also has the higher permit score. Thirty-five blocks move from Maldonado to Moreno and 23 move in the opposite direction; 43 move from Dowell to Cochran. Omitting the entire Moreno--Maldonado pair changes the pooled developer estimate from $-10.7\%$ to $-8.0\%$ ($p=0.158$). Omitting Dowell--Cochran gives $-8.6\%$ ($p=0.089$). Thus the negative sign persists, but significance at 5\% depends on individual comparisons.

Waguespack--Smith (32--43, 33 blocks) is the most consequential reversed pair. Waguespack has higher permit stringency, while Smith has the larger developer share, 0.60\% versus no identified developer funding for Waguespack. Removing that pair strengthens the negative developer estimate to $-13.2\%$. Burnett--Waguespack (27--32, nine blocks) goes the other way: Burnett has more developer funding, Waguespack higher permit stringency, and removing the pair weakens the developer estimate to $-9.8\%$. Reversed pairs therefore do not all push the pooled estimate in the same direction. Omitting any one informative pair leaves a negative developer estimate; these omissions are sensitivity checks, not additive contributions to Poisson.

The question is whether developer funding can plausibly proxy for regulatory leniency, not merely whether it yields a significant estimate. The current narrow classification can order aldermen using modest identified amounts: Pawar's \$3,500 of developer contributions (0.89\% of eligible dollars) exceeds the zero identified for Patrick O'Connor. A zero means no gifts matched the current developer rules, not proof that developers gave nothing. The donor classification was held fixed throughout this exercise. No substantive score definition or production result changed.
