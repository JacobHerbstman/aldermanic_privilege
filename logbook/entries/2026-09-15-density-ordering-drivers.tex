\section*{Burnett, other local comparisons, and density sensitivity}
\textit{September 15, 2026. Agent-drafted diagnosis requested by Jacob.}

Burnett's comparisons explain most of the change in multifamily DUPAC when
the adjusted-score ordering is replaced by raw average log processing time.
Switching only his eight disagreeing pairs, representing 113 projects,
moves the estimate from $-17.36\%$ to $+1.05\%$. Keeping his comparisons
in their original direction while switching everyone else gives
$-16.39\%$, versus $+2.16\%$ under the entirely raw ordering. The
Burnett--Fioretti pair alone, with 27 multifamily projects, moves the
estimate from $-17.36\%$ to $+0.14\%$ when reversed.

This does not mean that the negative estimate comes entirely from Burnett's
observations. Deleting all 157 projects in comparisons involving him,
including comparisons where the orderings agree, leaves $-14.49\%$ under
the original ordering. The corresponding raw-ordering estimate is
$-8.45\%$. For multifamily FAR, both Burnett and Hopkins matter:
switching only their respective disagreements gives $-3.46\%$ and
$-3.54\%$, compared with the original $-12.85\%$. Burnett--Fioretti,
Hopkins--Waguespack and Hopkins--Michele Smith are the largest individual
FAR switches. These changes cannot be added: the regression re-estimates
its other coefficients, and alderman groups overlap.

All-construction sensitivity is more dispersed. Dowell--King is the
largest individual switch for both FAR and DUPAC: it changes $-9.24\%$
to $-6.57\%$ and $-13.70\%$ to $-9.13\%$, respectively. Other large
changes involve Waguespack, Michele Smith, La Spata and Burnett--Fioretti.
Deleting all Burnett comparisons leaves all-construction FAR at $-8.93\%$
and DUPAC at $-12.96\%$. Thus the strong Burnett concentration is specific
to the multifamily ordering sensitivity, especially DUPAC.

\textit{A consequential unresolved data issue.} The Burnett--Fioretti
pair has four multifamily projects within 100 feet of the boundary. Three
are assigned to Burnett and one to Fioretti. Two of Burnett's records,
1100 and 1048 W Madison, each carry 81 units in the current data. Their
preserved 2021 Assessor reports have 9 and 24 units instead. Building and
land measurements are identical across these vintages: 16,094 and 4,599
square feet for 1100; 35,051 and 10,000 for 1048. The current DUPAC values
are 767.20 and 352.84. The source changed the counts; the code accepted the
newer report. The earlier review records the unit changes explicitly but
marks them resolved without separate permit unit evidence.

Recorded construction permits link these records to 4 and 3 N Aberdeen.
A YoChicago profile reports 9 and 18 units there, plus another 54 at
20--22 N Aberdeen. The original leasing agent confirms two 27-unit
buildings at 20 and 22; the buyer's acquisition announcement identifies
an 81-unit community. The evidence suggests that a community total was
repeated on individual-building measurements. The older Assessor count
of 24 also conflicts with the profile's 18, and the completion years need
reconciliation. These are strong reasons for building-level review, not
authorization to substitute unverified counts. Production values remain
unchanged. The full ordering sensitivity should not yet be interpreted
solely as a score-construction issue.
