\textit{Agent-drafted implementation of Jacob's address decision.}

Jacob confirmed that the Assessor's 1236 N Troy Street address is erroneous and
should be 1236 S Troy Street. Both Census and Chicago return South Troy when
queried with either direction. Chicago returns the same individual address point
with a score of 92.78 for the North query and 100 for South. New-construction
permit 100186300 also identifies South Troy. The earlier account of Chicago
having no accepted match referred to our score requirement; Chicago did return
the South address. Agreement between services was therefore stronger than that
account conveyed.

The correction is recorded by parcel number and original address in
\path{historical_address_corrections.csv}. The historical-address producer applies
this table before geocoding, preserving the original selected address and the
reason for the correction. It changes only Troy among 1,234 selected-address
records; every original field in the other records is unchanged. An unmatched
or stale decision fails explicitly. The scripts contain no Troy-specific address
or parcel-number condition. This is a recorded research decision, not an automatic
rule to change directions whenever two geocoders agree.

Chicago already takes precedence over Census for every construction observation
that needs address geocoding. An accepted Chicago match must be a unique individual
address point with score 100 and full street agreement. Census is the next address
source when that criterion fails. Historical and current exact-parcel coordinates
remain ahead of both services. Jacob's suggestion to try Chicago first therefore
retains the existing ordering; it does not replace parcel coordinates with address
estimates. The separate proposal to expand the use of historical exact-parcel
coordinates remains pending.

The corrected South query is preserved as an additional full response for each
service, keeping all original responses unchanged. The Chicago point lies in one
2008 parcel, 16241000360000. That polygon and the exact query are preserved as
additional source inputs for the preferred predecessor lookup. These changes
resolve the address disagreement and supply a historical parcel candidate; they
do not independently establish every aspect of the construction episode or its
final density measurement.
