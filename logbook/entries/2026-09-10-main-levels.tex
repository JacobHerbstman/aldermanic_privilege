\section*{Main results with outcomes in levels}
\textit{September 10, 2026. Agent-drafted exploratory comparison requested by Jacob.}

These regressions use the main paper's distance bands and controls but replace
log FAR, log DUPAC and log prices with their unlogged outcomes. Property controls
retain their existing functional forms. Permit results use linear count models
on the observations retained by each corresponding Poisson model. All fitted
samples match the originals; there is no additional trimming or winsorization.
These comparisons are separate from the cumulative-window exercise.

Immediately across the ward boundary, all-construction FAR is 0.167 lower
(SE 0.061, $p=0.007$) and DUPAC is 8.55 lower (SE 4.24, $p=0.046$).
Multifamily FAR is 0.382 lower (SE 0.211, $p=0.074$), while multifamily DUPAC
is 22.98 lower (SE 14.70, $p=0.122$). The all-construction pattern survives;
the multifamily DUPAC result is not statistically significant in levels.

The listed-rent difference is +\$38.90 per month (SE \$57.41, $p=0.499$),
and the sale-price difference is +\$703 (SE \$7,245, $p=0.923$), in 2022 dollars.
The sale-price 95\% interval runs from about -\$13,621 to +\$15,027.
The positive, significant log-price result therefore does not carry over to the
absolute-dollar specification on the same observations. Levels and logs target
different economic comparisons and respond differently to high-price properties;
this check alone does not identify the source of the difference.

The combined linear permit estimate is -0.054 permits per block-year
(SE 0.024, $p=0.025$). Separate directions give -0.093 for assignment toward
greater stringency ($p=0.011$) and +0.029 toward greater leniency ($p=0.330$).
The qualitative asymmetry in permit activity remains. These count differences
must not be described as percentage changes.
