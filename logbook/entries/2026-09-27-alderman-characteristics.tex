\section*{Alderman characteristics and the stringency score}
\textit{September 27, 2026. Agent-researched characteristics and agent-drafted comparison, requested by Jacob.}

A hand-researched table records, for all 113 aldermen in the 2006--2022 index, gender, race or ethnicity, birth year,
first year on the council, whether they were appointed, their occupation before the council, whether they were
lawyers or did property-tax or real-estate work, prior elected office, family political ties, Progressive Reform
Caucus membership, indictment or conviction for conduct related to office, and how they left. Each row lists its web
sources and notes; fields are left missing when no source documents them. Race is documented for 85 aldermen from
self-description, news descriptions, council caucus membership or documented ancestry; the other 28, mostly North
and Northwest Side aldermen, have no documented race and are not coded from surnames. Caucus membership agrees with
the caucus's published roster except for one alderman coded a member from other sources.

No characteristic predicts the score. Female aldermen score 0.16 SD lower, Black aldermen 0.06 lower and Hispanic
aldermen 0.08 higher, none significant. The largest differences are for prior elected office ($+0.30$ SD, $p=0.13$,
14 aldermen), indictment or conviction ($+0.53$, $p=0.23$, 9 aldermen) and Progressive Caucus membership ($-0.20$,
$p=0.35$). Together the characteristics explain 12 percent of the score's variation, and ward demographics, averaged
over each alderman's permits, are uncorrelated with it (all $|r|<0.08$). The score is not a proxy for aldermen's
observable backgrounds; with 113 aldermen and a score that is partly noise, the comparison has little power to detect
moderate relationships.

This complements the donation comparison of September 14, which found at most modest correlations with donor
composition. The characteristics are also intended for the planned alternative measures of alderman behavior.
