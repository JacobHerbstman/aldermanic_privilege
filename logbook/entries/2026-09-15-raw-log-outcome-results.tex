\section*{Outcome estimates using raw log processing times}
\textit{September 15, 2026. Agent-drafted analysis requested by Jacob.}

Ordering aldermen by raw average log permit processing time leaves the
permit and sale-price estimates close to their original values, but makes
the density estimates small and statistically imprecise. The permit effect
is $-11.80\%$ under raw ordering versus $-11.88\%$ under the adjusted score;
the corresponding sale-price estimates are $+3.17\%$ and $+3.04\%$. All
four estimates have conditional $p$-values below .05. For all construction,
FAR changes from $-9.24\%$ to $-1.84\%$ and DUPAC from $-13.70\%$ to
$+1.20\%$. For multifamily construction, FAR changes from $-12.85\%$ to
$-1.95\%$ and DUPAC from $-17.36\%$ to $+2.16\%$. The four raw-ordering
density estimates have $p$-values between .66 and .85. Rent changes from
$+1.76\%$ to $+1.30\%$, with neither distinguishable from zero at .10.

Jacob also requested dropping the disagreements. This retains only pairs
on which the adjusted score and raw log time agree. The permit effect is
$-12.42\%$ ($p=.0099$), sale prices are $+4.49\%$ ($p=.0116$), and rents
are $+3.20\%$ ($p=.0204$). All-construction FAR and DUPAC remain negative
at $-8.66\%$ and $-10.54\%$; multifamily FAR and DUPAC are $-7.05\%$ and
$-11.49\%$. All four density $p$-values exceed .10. The agreement-only
samples contain 2,657 construction projects, including 539 multifamily
projects, 41,290 sales and 121,615 rental observations. The permit sample
retains 3,153 blocks and 34,386 block-years, compared with 3,379 blocks and
36,830 block-years originally. This deletion changes sample composition,
particularly for rentals; it is not simply another ordering of the same data.

There are six disagreeing permit pairs, 73 all-construction pairs, 52
multifamily pairs, 101 sales pairs and 70 rental pairs. Reversing all these
disagreements is exactly equivalent to ordering every local comparison by
raw average log processing time. The audit checks that identity for each
record. It averages permit-level log durations, using exactly the same
eligible permits as each adjusted score: through 2014 for the remap and
through 2022 for boundary outcomes. No mean-log tie occurs in these pairs.
This is a coherent alternative ordering, unlike arbitrary pair reversals
that might conflict with one another.

The outcome specifications remain fixed. Boundary coefficients compare the
two 100-foot bands in the existing distance-bin model within 500 feet.
The permit effect pools 2015--2020 in the signed Poisson specification;
the annual path is also retained. Dropping a permit pair removes its
unchanged comparison blocks along with its reassigned blocks. Reversal
keeps unchanged blocks at zero. Joint pretrend $p$-values are .400 for
the original score, .435 after deletion and .483 under raw ordering.
These tests do not establish parallel trends.

The sensitivity differs substantially by outcome. This exercise does not
establish which ranking better measures alderman behavior: raw delays also
reflect permit composition, calendar time and location. Both orderings use
the same permits, and the reported intervals and $p$-values condition on
each sample and ordering. They exclude score-estimation uncertainty.
Production scores, datasets and manuscript files remain unchanged.
