\section*{Two tests of whether aldermen matter that do not use processing times}
\textit{September 27, 2026. Agent-drafted exploratory work on branch \texttt{alderman-effects}, requested by Jacob
after he asked for alternatives to the processing-time index.}

\textit{Alderman effects on permit applications.} A citywide panel of every 2010 census block for 2006--2022 (787,287
block-years) records high- and low-discretion permit applications and the alderman serving the block for most of each
year; its counts match the event-study panel exactly on the 359,579 block-years they share. Poisson regressions with
block and year fixed effects give each of 110 aldermen an effect on applications, identified by the 2015 remap and
turnover; a second design adds 2003-ward-by-year fixed effects, so only blocks of one old ward represented by
different aldermen identify the effects. Beyond sampling noise, alderman effects spread by 0.155 log points for
high-discretion applications and 0.037 for low-discretion ones (0.135 and zero in the second design), and the two
designs rank aldermen alike (Spearman 0.92). But the effects do not persist over an alderman's tenure: estimated from
two interleaved halves of each ward's blocks they correlate at 0.55, from odd and even years at only 0.14. They look
like ward-period development activity credited to whoever is serving, which one remap and turnover cannot separate
from alderman behavior. They are also unrelated to the processing-time index (Spearman $-0.03$).

\textit{Jumps at ward boundaries in either direction.} Labeling each side by ward number and estimating the jump
within each boundary segment and pair of facing aldermen, jumps in listed rents vary across cells about as much at
real boundaries as at placebo lines 1,000 feet inside a ward (variance 0.0127, SE 0.0016, against 0.0104 and 0.0108).
When the facing aldermen change, a segment's rent jump changes somewhat more at real boundaries (0.0069, SE 0.0019)
than at placebo lines (0.0024 and 0.0046), by one to two standard errors. Sales jumps are indistinguishable from
placebo jumps. The density samples are too small for the test.

Neither test provides a stringency ranking better than the index. What survives is narrower: permit activity
differs more across aldermen for high-discretion than low-discretion permits, and rent discontinuities may shift
somewhat when facing aldermen change. Building the 2006--2009 and 2021--2022 block counts required 48 more
hand-reviewed drops of permits outside every block, added to \path{tasks/permit_block_hand_adjudications}.

\textit{Reproduction.} Run \texttt{make} in \path{tasks/estimate_alderman_permit_effects/code/} and
\path{tasks/test_boundary_heterogeneity/code/} on branch \texttt{alderman-effects}; the task READMEs describe the
designs and standard errors.
