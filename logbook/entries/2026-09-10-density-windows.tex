\section*{Density comparisons across wider boundary windows}
\textit{September 10, 2026. Agent-drafted exploratory results; not adopted as the paper specification.}

Jacob requested a comparison of all construction within 100, 200, 300, 400 and
500 feet on each side of the ward boundary. Each regression replaces the paper's
signed distance-band indicators with one more-stringent-side indicator, retaining
its neighborhood controls and zoning-group, boundary-segment and construction-year
fixed effects. Controls are re-estimated within each window, and standard errors
are clustered by ward pair. There are no distance slopes. This estimates an
adjusted difference across the entire window rather than the paper's comparison
between the two nearest 100-foot bands.

For all construction, FAR is 12.5\% lower within 100 feet and 12.2\% lower within
200 feet. DUPAC is 14.7\% and 12.4\% lower, respectively. All four estimates are
significant at 5\%. At 300--500 feet the differences are small and statistically
indistinguishable from zero; at 500 feet the estimates are +1.3\% for FAR and
+0.1\% for DUPAC. The wider averages therefore do not establish uniformly lower
density throughout the full boundary strip.

Multifamily estimates are less stable. At 100 feet, FAR and DUPAC are 44.5\% and
39.6\% lower, but the samples contain only 193 and 194 construction observations.
At 500 feet the differences are -4.5\% and -8.0\%, neither significant at 5\%.
Changing the window changes both the observations and the fitted controls, so
this exercise does not isolate which change explains the large narrow-window
multifamily estimate. The main paper specification remains unchanged.

match the log models. Within 100 feet, all-construction FAR is 0.212 lower and
DUPAC is 8.42 lower on the more-stringent side, both significant at 5\%.

\paragraph{September 11: common-sample update.}
Jacob requested the average difference over the full 500-foot range on each
side, using the newly adopted common FAR and DUPAC sample. The preceding
numerical discussion describes the earlier outcome-specific samples; the linked
figures now use the common sample. At 500 feet the adjusted differences are
$+1.34\%$ for all-project FAR ($p=0.631$), $+0.18\%$ for all-project DUPAC
($p=0.966$), $-4.45\%$ for multifamily FAR ($p=0.229$), and $-8.07\%$ for
multifamily DUPAC ($p=0.126$). None is significant at 10 percent. Both outcomes
use 4,023 projects overall and 863 multifamily projects.

These regressions replace the distance-band indicators with one indicator for
the more-stringent side, retaining the main controls, fixed effects and
ward-pair clustering. They estimate an adjusted difference over the full
window, rather than averaging the plotted distance-band coefficients. The
corresponding direct level differences are $+0.017$ FAR and $-0.390$ DUPAC
overall, and $-0.031$ FAR and $-6.631$ DUPAC for multifamily; none is significant
at 10 percent. The manuscript and its production figures are unchanged.

\paragraph{Straight boundaries.}
Jacob also requested the same comparison for the paper's locally straight
boundaries. The sample contains 3,140 projects overall and 625 multifamily
projects. The existing nearest-band estimates are $-7.78\%$ FAR ($p=0.081$)
and $-11.71\%$ DUPAC ($p=0.059$) overall, and $-10.35\%$ FAR ($p=0.183$)
and $-14.91\%$ DUPAC ($p=0.187$) for multifamily. The whole-window averages
are $+1.69\%$ FAR ($p=0.349$) and $+1.17\%$ DUPAC ($p=0.674$) overall,
and $-6.59\%$ FAR ($p=0.158$) and $-10.42\%$ DUPAC ($p=0.131$) for
multifamily. None of the whole-window estimates is significant at 10 percent.
The straight-boundary estimate file and its standard report contain 20 models
using the same common FAR/DUPAC eligibility, controls and clustering. The
manuscript remains unchanged.
