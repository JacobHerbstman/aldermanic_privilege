\section*{Density comparisons across wider boundary windows}
\textit{September 10, 2026. Agent-drafted exploratory results; not adopted as the paper specification.}

Jacob requested a comparison of all construction within 100, 200, 300, 400 and
500 feet on each side of the ward boundary. Each regression replaces the paper's
signed distance-band indicators with one more-stringent-side indicator, retaining
its neighborhood controls and zoning-group, boundary-segment and construction-year
fixed effects. Controls are re-estimated within each window, and standard errors
are clustered by ward pair. There are no distance slopes. This estimates an
adjusted difference across the entire window rather than the paper's comparison
between the two nearest 100-foot bands.

For all construction, FAR is 12.5\% lower within 100 feet and 12.2\% lower within
200 feet. DUPAC is 14.7\% and 12.4\% lower, respectively. All four estimates are
significant at 5\%. At 300--500 feet the differences are small and statistically
indistinguishable from zero; at 500 feet the estimates are +1.3\% for FAR and
+0.1\% for DUPAC. The wider averages therefore do not establish uniformly lower
density throughout the full boundary strip.

Multifamily estimates are less stable. At 100 feet, FAR and DUPAC are 44.5\% and
39.6\% lower, but the samples contain only 193 and 194 construction observations.
At 500 feet the differences are -4.5\% and -8.0\%, neither significant at 5\%.
Changing the window changes both the observations and the fitted controls, so
this exercise does not isolate which change explains the large narrow-window
multifamily estimate. The main paper specification remains unchanged.
