\section*{Multifamily density is sensitive to uncertain local score orderings}
\textit{September 14, 2026. Agent-drafted exploratory checks requested by Jacob.}

Uncertainty about which alderman is more stringent materially changes the
multifamily density estimates, especially DUPAC. The existing common FAR/DUPAC
sample contains 863 buildings and 161 named alderman comparisons. Its estimated
density differences are $-12.9\%$ for FAR and $-17.4\%$ for DUPAC, with
conditional clustered $p$-values of 0.053 and 0.081. These estimates reproduce
the recorded common-sample benchmark to numerical precision. All experiments
retain the paper's two-stage score construction and original density
specification: the comparison within 100 feet on either side, ten distance
bins within 500 feet, five own-ward demographic controls, zoning-group,
boundary-segment and construction-year fixed effects, and geographic ward-pair
clustering.

We first define fragile comparisons using only the permit-score draws,
before inspecting density effects. The 43 pairs that preserve their ordering
in fewer than 75\% of annual draws account for 210 buildings. Dropping them
leaves 653 buildings and effects of $-10.3\%$ for FAR and $-7.7\%$ for DUPAC
($p=0.232$ and $0.488$). Keeping the buildings but reversing all 43 orderings
gives $-7.7\%$ and $+2.6\%$. With the broader threshold of 95\% retention,
91 pairs and 418 buildings are affected: dropping them gives $-14.0\%$ and
$-8.3\%$, while reversing them gives $-2.0\%$ and $+4.6\%$. All methods and
both thresholds remain in the report. Dropping changes the represented
population. Reversing selected pairs is a deliberate stress test and can
conflict with a single citywide ranking; it is not an adopted alternative
score. These conditional intervals and $p$-values treat the tested sample
and ordering as fixed.

Every pair is also dropped and reversed individually, including pairs with
stable scores. Fioretti--Burnett, representing 27 buildings along wards 2 and
27, is the largest sensitivity. The paper's score orders Fioretti as more
stringent; that order persists in 64.1\% of annual draws. Dropping this pair
gives $-9.6\%$ for FAR and $-9.7\%$ for DUPAC. Reversing it gives $-7.3\%$
and $+0.1\%$. Neither density estimate changes sign under any single-pair
deletion; across all 161 deletions, FAR ranges from $-15.6\%$ to $-9.6\%$
and DUPAC from $-21.7\%$ to $-9.7\%$. Stable comparisons also have influence:
Taliaferro--Villegas retains its order in 98.2\% of annual draws, yet
reversing that pair moves FAR to $-7.4\%$ and DUPAC to $-10.7\%$.

We also carry all 499 score vectors from each of the property, quarter, and
annual reweightings through both regressions: 2,994 refits in total. Each
draw provides a coherent citywide ordering; buildings, outcomes, controls,
locations, and absolute distances stay fixed. Under annual reweighting, the
median effects are $-10.1\%$ for FAR and $-12.9\%$ for DUPAC. The middle
95\% of draws range from $-19.2\%$ to $+3.5\%$ and from $-23.6\%$ to
$+8.3\%$, respectively. The estimated effects remain negative in 92.4\% and
81.4\% of annual draws. These are descriptive score-only ranges and draw
shares, not full confidence intervals or probabilities that the causal
effect is negative. Property and quarter reweighting give more stable FAR
signs, while their DUPAC ranges also cross zero. Annual reweighting has only
17 time blocks. None of these checks resolves the interpretation of permit
delays or sensitivity to joint estimation of neighborhood and alderman effects.

\textit{Reproduction and scope.} Run \texttt{make} in
\path{tasks/audits/multifamily_score_uncertainty/code/}. The audit reads the
recorded construction benchmark and score draws documented in
\path{tasks/audits/score_pair_reliability/}; the upstream implementation was
committed at \texttt{7dd04922}. Its two scripts estimate the tests and display
them. Saved tables retain every draw, every grouped stress test, all 161
one-pair comparisons, and source-score retention shares. Keys, missingness,
sample equality, and a reversal of all boundaries are checked. A full
reversal negates both baseline coefficients and preserves their standard
errors. This work does not resample building outcomes or estimate combined
score-and-outcome uncertainty. Production scores, data, results, and
manuscript files are unchanged.
