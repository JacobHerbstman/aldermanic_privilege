\section*{Sale-price and rent sensitivity to uncertain score orderings}
\textit{September 15, 2026. Agent-drafted exploratory checks requested by Jacob.}

Sale-price and rent differences remain positive in almost all complete score
draws. Their point estimates are less sensitive to score resampling than
multifamily DUPAC, but conventional estimation uncertainty remains important,
especially for rents. The original estimates are $+3.04\%$ for sales
($p=0.027$) and $+1.76\%$ for rents ($p=0.268$). The audit reproduces both
recorded benchmarks exactly for the coefficient, standard error, p-value,
and fitted sample: 58,468 sales across 367 named alderman pairs and 240,713
rental observations across 244 pairs. Rental observations can repeat for a
home over time; the count is not distinct homes.

Using the same score-only fragility thresholds as the density and permit
audits, 81 sales pairs have less than 75\% annual ordering retention. They
represent 14,560 sales. Dropping them yields $+2.88\%$ ($p=0.112$), while
reversing their orderings gives $+1.18\%$ ($p=0.458$). At the broader 95\%
threshold, dropping 202 pairs and 34,907 sales gives $+4.32\%$, and
reversing them gives $+0.38\%$; neither is statistically significant at 10\%.
For rents, the 75\% threshold identifies 63 pairs and 52,747 observations.
Dropping them gives $+2.41\%$ ($p=0.147$), and reversing gives $+2.32\%$
($p=0.097$). The 95\% annual threshold identifies 142 pairs and 97,219
observations; dropping gives $+2.07\%$ and reversing gives $+1.29\%$.

The complete score draws preserve the positive direction more consistently
than broad, deliberate reversals. Across 499 annual draws, the median sale
effect is $+2.05\%$, with middle 95\% range $[+0.48,+3.91]\%$ and 98.8\%
positive estimates. The median rent effect is $+1.89\%$, with range
$[+0.69,+2.89]\%$ and 99.4\% positive estimates. Property and quarter
reweighting yield sales medians of $+2.23\%$ and $+2.29\%$, with 98.6\%
and 98.8\% positive draws. Rent medians are $+1.81\%$ and $+1.92\%$, with
100\% and 99.4\% positive draws. These ranges describe score uncertainty
alone, holding observed prices fixed. They are not full confidence intervals
and do not establish a statistically significant rental effect. The draw
shares are not probabilities that the causal effect is positive.

All 611 market-specific pairs are tested individually, including stable
ones. Every individual sales deletion or reversal preserves the positive
sign. Deletion estimates range from $+2.43\%$ to $+3.59\%$, while reversals
range from $+1.79\%$ to $+4.09\%$. JoAnn Thompson--Willie Cochran is the
largest sales sensitivity: its 321 sales have 100\% ordering retention in
all three resampling methods, yet reversing that pair lowers the estimate
to $+1.79\%$. For rents, every individual deletion remains positive, from
$+1.05\%$ to $+2.41\%$. Reversing Reilly--Hopkins, representing 21,216
rental observations and 98.2\% annual ordering retention, gives $-0.31\%$;
dropping it gives $+1.05\%$. These are influential comparisons whose score
orderings are relatively stable.

The full report retains unfavorable grouped tests. Reversing all sales
pairs with less than 95\% property or quarter retention produces $-0.70\%$
and $-0.62\%$, respectively; those sets contain 207 and 213 pairs. Such
deliberate reversals can conflict with a coherent citywide ranking. They
are stress tests, not adopted scores. Dropping changes the population
represented, and conditional p-values treat each chosen sample and
ordering as fixed. No preferred specification is selected from these tests.

\textit{Reproduction and scope.} Run \texttt{make} in
\path{tasks/audits/price_score_uncertainty/code/}. The audit reads the recorded
sales and rent panels under \path{data_raw/score_robustness/}, with source
hashes in the score-reliability audit. It consumes that audit's through-2022
score vectors (upstream implementation \texttt{7dd04922}) rather than
estimating new scores. Both regressions use the original strict 500-foot
window and ten 100-foot bins, comparing $[0,100)$ with $[-100,0)$.
Sales retain 2006--2022 dates and segment-by-quarter fixed effects; rents
retain 2014--2022 dates and segment-by-month fixed effects. Property
characteristics, amenity controls, property-type indicators and ward-pair
clustering are unchanged.

One script estimates each market; one renders the report. The saved outputs
contain 2,994 score-draw refits, 1,248 baseline and stress-test rows, all
market-specific pair counts and retention shares, and 1,222 individual-pair
results. Source-score direction, key uniqueness and matched fitted rows are
checked. Reversing all boundaries negates each coefficient and preserves
its standard error. In the completed runs, deleting pairs caused no further
fixed-effect observation loss beyond the selected records. The report and
figure rebuild through Make; a missing figure was regenerated with unchanged
CSV, HTML and PNG bytes. Production data, scores and manuscript are unchanged.
Combined score-and-outcome uncertainty and sensitivity to estimating the
score controls jointly remain separate questions.
