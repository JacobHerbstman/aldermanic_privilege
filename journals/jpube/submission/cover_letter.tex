\begin{letter}{Editors\\
Journal of Public Economics}

\opening{Dear Editors,}

I am submitting ``Informal Regulation and Housing Supply: Evidence from
Chicago's Aldermanic Privilege'' for consideration as a Short Paper at the
\textit{Journal of Public Economics}. I would also welcome consideration under
the journal's regular review track if the editors believe that track is more
appropriate.

The paper studies whether informal political discretion affects housing supply
even when formal land-use rules are shared citywide. Chicago's aldermanic
privilege provides a setting in which each of the city's 50 council members exercises
substantial informal control over development in a single ward. I construct
alderman-level stringency scores from permit processing times and combine two
research designs. An event study uses the 2015 ward redistricting to compare
permit activity within census blocks assigned to different aldermen. A
boundary-discontinuity design compares completed construction and housing costs
immediately across ward boundaries.

Assignment to a more stringent alderman lowers high-discretion permitting
activity by 12 percent, with no comparable change in low-discretion permits.
Multifamily projects built on the more-stringent side of a ward boundary have
13 percent lower floor area ratios and 17 percent fewer dwelling units per
acre. Home prices are 3.0 percent higher on the more-stringent side, while rents
are 1.8 percent higher but imprecisely estimated.

The results show how individual politicians can create different regulatory
environments within one municipal government and affect both permitting and
completed housing supply. They speak to work in public economics on local
political control, regulatory design, and housing supply.

The main text contains fewer than 6,000 words and five exhibits: four figures
and one table. For ease of review, I am submitting the main text and appendix
together in a single manuscript. If accepted, the appendix would appear
separately as an online appendix.

The manuscript is original, has not been published, and is not under
consideration elsewhere.

Thank you for your consideration.

\closing{Sincerely,}
